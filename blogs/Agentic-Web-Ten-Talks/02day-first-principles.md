# Agentic Web — Ten Talks · Day 2: Why Do AI Agents Need an Open Internet?

> **Core argument**: An open Internet enables a better experience—AI can make more intelligent decisions only when it has access to complete context, and it can complete more tasks only when it can call every relevant service.

On Day 1, we reached a key judgment: **in an era when humans were the primary productive force, a closed Internet was the optimal solution.**

But that conclusion contains a hidden premise: **humans are the primary productive force, and the Internet is designed for humans to use.** What happens if that premise changes?

---

## I. The Essence of an Agent: Deciding and Acting on Your Behalf

Traditional software—WeChat, Taobao, and Twitter—is essentially a **tool**. It extends human capabilities, but the decision-making power remains with humans.

**AI agents are different. They do not “help you click buttons”; they “decide and act on your behalf.”**

When you say, “Help me book a flight to Shanghai tomorrow,” you are not asking for a list of flights to choose from—you expect the AI to book the ticket for you.

This requires two core capabilities:

**Decision-making requires complete context.** A truly intelligent AI should not repeatedly ask about your budget or preferred airline. It should be able to access your financial situation to determine a budget, review your past orders to understand your preferences, and read your calendar to confirm the timing.

**Execution requires calling services.** Even after making the perfect decision, AI is still only an “advisor” if it cannot execute. It must be able to call booking, payment, and calendar interfaces directly and complete the task from end to end.

**Without complete context, AI cannot make good decisions; without the ability to call services, it cannot complete tasks.**

---

## II. The Two Shackles of the Closed Internet

### Shackle One: Cutting Off Context

You ask AI to recommend a restaurant, and it gives you a list of highly rated places—but one of them is a restaurant you visited last week and left a negative review for on another platform. Why recommend somewhere you clearly dislike? Because AI can see only the data on the current platform.

You and your friends discuss your Spring Festival travel plans on WeChat: Sanya, from the third through the sixth day of the lunar new year, with a budget of 5,000 yuan per person. When you open a travel app to book, its AI assistant asks, “Where do you want to go? When are you leaving? What is your budget?” Why? Because the discussion happened on WeChat, which the travel app cannot see.

**Data silos cut off context, leaving agents able to make only a “local optimum,” not a “global optimum.”**

### Shackle Two: Blocking Service Calls

The predicament facing most AI today is that it can tell you what to do, but it cannot do it for you.

It can recommend a restaurant, but you still have to open an app and make the reservation yourself. It can generate a travel plan, but you must carry out every step manually.

**At its core, this is still “a search engine plus a recommendation system,” not a true intelligent assistant.**

Why can't agents call services directly? There are two walls—one technical and one commercial:

1. **UIs are designed for humans**: having AI simulate clicks is a transitional solution; it is inefficient, fragile, and impossible to scale.
2. **Platform interests conflict**: opening interfaces to external agents would undermine the foundations of the platforms themselves.

---

## III. A Scenario Comparison: Planning a Business Trip to Shanghai

### A Human on the Closed Internet

Open Ctrip to search for flights, Meituan to search for hotels, Dianping to search for restaurants, Amap to check transportation, and a calendar to record the itinerary…

**One hour, five or six apps, and more than a dozen switches. Tedious, but doable.**

### AI on the Closed Internet

It can search for flights but cannot book them; it can recommend hotels but does not know where the meeting is; it can list restaurants but does not know your taste preferences. Every step requires human intervention.

**The experience is even worse than doing it yourself**—because you not only have to do the work yourself, but first explain your needs to AI and then manually execute its suggestions one by one.

### AI on the Open Internet

You say one sentence: “Arrange my business trip to Shanghai next Tuesday. I have a meeting in Pudong, and I would like to eat Shanghai cuisine in the evening.”

The agent simultaneously reads your calendar to confirm the time and location, checks your past orders to understand your preferences, checks your loyalty accounts, calls the relevant interfaces to make the bookings, and syncs the itinerary to your calendar.

**A few seconds, and everything is done.**

**For humans, a closed Internet is “inconvenient”; for agents, it is “unusable.”**

---

## IV. Experience, Cost, and Efficiency: Why We Believe Openness Is Inevitable

The greatest resistance to an open Internet often comes from today's platform landscape: platforms are powerful enough that, in the short term, they have little incentive to “give away” their service capabilities. That would be tantamount to cutting their own lifeline.

But we believe openness will ultimately emerge not because of a value position or idealism, but because of a more fundamental judgment: in the long run, technology and business are guided by **experience, cost, and efficiency**.

When LLMs enter an open ecosystem, fragmented context and services can be linked into continuous workflows—users no longer have to switch back and forth among multiple apps, so the experience improves; repetitive manual operations can be automated, reducing the cost in time; and more tasks can move from “recommendation” to “completion,” increasing overall efficiency. This is the deeper force behind openness.

The evolution will of course face resistance. But if super-apps are unwilling to open up, new companies will be willing to use open approaches to disrupt the old ecosystems.

---

## V. In Closing: Advanced Productive Forces Need Matching Relations of Production

The reasoning chain across the first two days:

> **Day 1**: In an era when humans were the primary productive force, closed platforms were the optimal solution for efficiency.

> **Day 2**: Agents need complete context to make decisions and access to all relevant services to execute—and the closed Internet cuts off both.

The contradiction is now visible: **old productive forces gave rise to a closed Internet; new productive forces demand an open Internet.**

AI is an advanced productive force. Only an open Internet can unleash its capabilities. We are not choosing openness; the new form of productive force is demanding it. The conflict between AI and the existing closed ecosystem has already begun—for example, in the recent blocking of Doubao's phone assistant.
